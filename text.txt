The Amazon rainforest is the largest tropical rainforest on the planet.
It covers much of the Amazon basin in South America.
This basin spans nine countries, with most of the forest located in Brazil.
The rainforest is home to an incredible diversity of plants and animals.
Scientists estimate that millions of species live within its borders.
Many of these species have not yet been discovered or studied.
The forest also plays a critical role in regulating the global climate.
Its trees absorb enormous amounts of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere.
For this reason it is often called the lungs of the planet.
However, deforestation has become a serious threat in recent decades.
Large areas of forest are cleared every year for farming and cattle ranching.
This destruction releases stored carbon and reduces biodiversity.
Conservation efforts aim to protect the remaining forest and its inhabitants.
Protecting the Amazon is essential for the health of the entire planet.
